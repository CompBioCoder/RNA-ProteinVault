PubMed · 2026-10-13 · J Ethnopharmacol · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869395/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Elucidating the mechanisms underlying realgar-induced toxicity is essential for the early prevention and treatment of arsenic-related adverse effects and for reducing the associated healthcare burden. Owing to its poor solubility, realgar preferentially accumulates in the intestine after oral administration. This study aimed to elucidate the molecular mechanism by which realgar regulates exosomal miR-125a-5p (exo-miR-125a-5p) sorting in colonic epithelial cells and to investigate the role of exo-miR-125a-5p in realgar-induced central nervous system (CNS) toxicity. A chronic realgar-exposed mouse model was established and combined with AAV9-mediated hnRNPU knockdown, miR-125a-5p-enriched exosome administration, and probiotic intervention. Complementary in vitro studies were performed using YAMC cells subjected to hnRNPU silencing or co-treatment with realgar and L-cysteine sulfinic acid (LCSA). Small RNA sequencing, 16S rDNA sequencing, quantitative proteomics, untargeted metabolomics, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), miRNA pull-down, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), Western blotting, and other molecular biological approaches were integrated to systematically investigate the molecular mechanism underlying realgar-regulated exo-miR-125a-5p sorting in colonic epithelial cells and its contribution to realgar-induced CNS toxicity. Realgar exposure induced dose-dependent colonic injury, altered the exosomal miRNA profile of colonic epithelial cells, and significantly reduced the secretion of exo-miR-125a-5p. Mechanistically, the RNA-binding protein hnRNPU directly bound mature miR-125a-5p, enhanced its post-transcriptional stability, and promoted its nuclear retention, thereby limiting its loading into exosomes. Realgar-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis reduced the abundance of Lactobacillus and the level of its metabolite, LCSA, resulting in aberrant upregulation of hnRNPU. The consequent reduction in circulating exo-miR-125a-5p relieved repression of the STAT1 signaling pathway, leading to activation of the microglial STAT1-NLRP3 inflammasome axis and exacerbation of realgar-induced CNS toxicity. Realgar induces both colonic and central nervous system toxicity. Mechanistically, realgar disrupts gut microbial homeostasis, depleting Lactobacillus-derived LCSA, which promotes hnRNPU expression and enhances the nuclear retention of mature miR-125a-5p. This process suppresses miR-125a-5p sorting into exosomes, reduces circulating exo-miR-125a-5p levels, and consequently activates the microglial STAT1-NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, thereby aggravating CNS toxicity. These findings identify a previously unrecognized gut microbiota-metabolite-RNA-binding protein axis governing exosomal miRNA sorting and establish exo-miR-125a-5p as a critical mediator linking intestinal injury to neuroinflammation. This study provides new mechanistic insights into realgar-induced colonic and CNS toxicity and expands our understanding of gut-brain axis-mediated toxicological mechanisms.
