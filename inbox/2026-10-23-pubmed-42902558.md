PubMed · 2026-10-20 · Plant Commun · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42902558/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Sugar signaling governs carbon allocation between source and sink organs in plants, and excess sugar is known to repress transcripts of photosynthesis and sugar-metabolism genes. How this regulation operates post-transcriptionally remains unclear. Here we study RGGA, a RGG-repeat RNA-binding protein, and its interaction with the 5'-3' exoribonuclease XRN4 to understand how selective mRNA degradation contributes to sugar-responsive growth. In loss-of-function and domain-deletion analyses, RGGA promotes selective degradation of a defined mRNA subset, including genes involved in sucrose transport/metabolism, chlorophyll biosynthesis, and root development. In rgga mutants, these transcripts exhibit markedly extended half-lives. Disruption of the RGGA-XRN4 interaction, either by RGGA loss or deletion of the XRN4-binding domain, prevents XRN4 from recognizing these targets, leading to robust upregulation of the corresponding mRNAs. Phenotypically, rgga mutants show enhanced growth under exogenous sucrose, with a detectable growth boost after only a 5-day sucrose supply during the seedling stage. This growth pattern aligns with upregulated sugar-transport/metabolism and developmental programs driven by stabilized transcripts. These results suggest that RGGA functions as a sugar responsive cofactor that guides XRN4-mediated selective mRNA degradation, shaping post-transcriptional gene expression and plant growth in response to sugar availability. Prolonged stability of specific mRNAs promotes sugar-dependent growth, revealing a mechanism by which plants modulate carbon allocation via mRNA turnover. Targeting RGGA could help enhance crop growth under variable sugar conditions without broad transcriptional changes.
