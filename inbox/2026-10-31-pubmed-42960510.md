> [!abstract] Abstract
> RNA structures that form inside living cells govern processes ranging from translation to RNA decay, many of which are controlled by RNA-binding proteins (RBPs). Because RBP specificity can depend on both RNA sequence and folding, traditional pulldown-based methods often obscure the structural context of bound RNAs. Here, a quantitative framework based on dimethyl sulfate mutational profiling and sequencing was established to directly measure RNA structure and RBP binding in human cells at single-nucleotide resolution. Applied to a 1600-member MS2 hairpin library, this approach revealed that RNA folding is the strongest determinant of MS2 coat protein (MCP) recognition in the MS2-MCP system, requiring formation of a stable hairpin and a consensus loop sequence, with stem length or GC content contributing primarily through their effect on hairpin folding. An in-cell occupancy model was derived to quantify fractional binding from structural readouts. Complementary systems using degron-tuned MCP and a downregulatory MCP fusion demonstrated binding behavior under subsaturating protein concentrations and showed that inferred occupancy quantitatively predicts RNA degradation efficiency. Together, these results establish a quantitative strategy for measuring RBP-RNA specificity and affinity directly in cells, using the MS2-MCP system to link RNA structure, protein occupancy, and regulatory outcome.
