> [!abstract] Abstract
> Aminoglycosides such as neomycin are clinically limited by toxicity, commonly attributed to the inhibition of mitoribosomes. Here, we investigate this model by integrating Cryo-EM and functional assays to assess neomycin's binding landscape and functional consequences for human ribosomes. Cryo-EM reveals extensive multi-site binding of neomycin on both human mitochondrial and cytosolic ribosomes in vitro, including conserved regions of the decoding center. In contrast, ribosomes isolated from neomycin-treated cells show markedly different patterns, with no detectable neomycin density on the mitoribosomes and reduced occupancy at a subset of cytosolic ribosome sites. Functional assays indicate that neomycin remained active in cells, inducing widespread stop-codon readthrough while having minimal effects on global mitochondrial and cytosolic protein synthesis. Together, these findings highlight a disconnect between the structural binding landscape and ribosome-associated neomycin recovered following cellular exposure, while linking neomycin exposure to altered translational fidelity with minimal effects on global protein synthesis.
