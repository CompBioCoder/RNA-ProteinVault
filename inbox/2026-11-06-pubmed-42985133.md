> [!abstract] Abstract
> Long intergenic non-coding RNAs (lincRNAs) have emerged as important regulators of plant immune responses, yet their functional landscape in wheat (Triticum aestivum) during stripe rust (Puccinia striiformis f. sp. tritici) infection remains largely unexplored. In this study, a genome-wide identification of lincRNAs was performed using strand-specific RNA-seq data from wheat leaves following Pst inoculation. Functional enrichment, co-expression, lincRNA-miRNA-mRNA network prediction, and AlphaFold-based protein-RNA structure prediction were used to characterize candidate lincRNAs. A total of 2,736 expressed lincRNAs were identified, of which 515 were differentially expressed during infection. Functional enrichment of genes located within 100 kb of the differentially expressed lincRNAs revealed significant associations with defense-related pathways, including MAPK signaling and plant-pathogen interaction. Coordinated expression patterns between lincRNAs and adjacent defense-related mRNAs suggested putative cis-regulatory associations. Furthermore, lincRNA-miRNA-mRNA network prediction identified 65 differentially expressed lincRNAs as potential endogenous target mimics for 21 conserved miRNAs, revealing a widespread competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory network. Importantly, AlphaFold-based protein-RNA structure prediction demonstrated that the co-expressed lincRNA MSTRG.56980 forms a stable complex with the RGA1-type resistance protein TraesCS6B03G0179100. This provides a first computational prediction of a possible physical interaction between a wheat lincRNA and an immune receptor. These integrative findings substantially expand the current understanding of non-coding regulatory networks in wheat immunity and provide a foundational resource for prioritizing candidate lincRNAs as targets for enhancing stripe rust resistance.
