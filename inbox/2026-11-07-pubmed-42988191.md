> [!abstract] Abstract
> Cold and drought are major abiotic stresses that severely constrain rice (Oryza sativa) growth and productivity. Cold shock proteins (CSPs) act as RNA chaperones that facilitate post-transcriptional adaptation under environmental stress. In this study, we combined phylogenetic analysis, prokaryotic heterologous expression, and all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to elucidate the evolutionary features, stress-protective capacity, and working mechanism of rice OsCSP1. Promoter analysis revealed enrichment of low-temperature responsive (LTR) and MYB-binding drought-responsive (MBS) cis-elements upstream of OsCSP1. Heterologous overexpression of OsCSP1 in Escherichia coli showed no cytotoxicity at 37°C. Under 17°C cold stress, recombinant strains overcame initial metabolic burden and exhibited superior biomass accumulation (OD600) compared with empty vector controls during late log phase. Spot dilution assays indicated enhanced colony-forming ability at 10-3 dilution. Sector-plate assays further demonstrated strong induction-dependent cytoprotection against both prolonged cold (17°C) and 15% (w/v) PEG6000-induced osmotic stress in E. coli, highlighting its potential protective efficacy in heterologous systems. AlphaFold 3 modeling of the OsCSP1-U6 RNA complex yielded a moderate interface confidence (ipTM = 0.66), which is expected for proteins containing intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs). Subsequent 100-ns all-atom MD simulations revealed rapid contraction in radius of gyration (Rg) and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA), forming a compact, solvent-resistant conformation. RMSF analysis showed a rigid N-terminal CSD anchor coupled with a highly dynamic C-terminal IDR. MM/PBSA binding free energy decomposition indicated that the interaction is primarily driven by van der Waals and electrostatic forces. We propose a "rigid-anchor, flexible-melting" model in which the CSD is predicted to provide stable RNA binding while the flexible IDR is hypothesized to facilitate the destabilization of misfolded RNA secondary structures. These findings provide a foundational biophysical framework that facilitates future in planta functional validation of OsCSP1 as a potential target for engineering climate-resilient rice.
