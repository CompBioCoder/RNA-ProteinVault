> [!abstract] Abstract
> Human parainfluenza virus type 2 (HPIV2) is an orthorubulavirus respiratory pathogen whose genome is encapsidated by the viral nucleoprotein (N), for which high-resolution experimental structural information has been lacking. Here, we report a cryo-electron microscopy structure of recombinant HPIV2 N assembled with co-purified RNA in a closed, near-planar 13-protomer ring, reconstructed with C13 symmetry at a nominal, sampling-limited resolution of 2.91 Å. Tilted cryo-EM data collection was used to mitigate strong preferred orientation, and C13-symmetric refinement of 430,693 particles yielded this reconstruction. An independent C1 reconstruction supported the closed ring-like architecture without imposed rotational symmetry. The structure contains a conserved RNA-binding groove. Because the recombinant protein co-purified with heterogeneous host-derived RNA and the groove-associated density was not sequence-resolved, a template-derived poly-uridine strand was positioned as a conservative representation of a plausible RNA path and six-nucleotide-per-protomer register rather than as a native RNA assignment. Contact-map and ILV/LIV hydrophobic-cluster analyses identified recurrent intra-protomer and inter-protomer packing features, including N-arm-to-neighbouring-core and C-terminal-side contacts. In single 300 ns RNA-bound and RNA-free molecular-dynamics trajectories, the overall ring architecture and compactness were maintained, while local flexibility differences were observed near the RNA-binding groove. Comparative structural analysis places the recombinant HPIV2 N 13-mer observed here among near-planar paramyxovirus N assemblies, alongside PIV5 and the near-planar mumps N form, and distinguishes it from more strongly pitched nucleocapsid assemblies. These results define the oligomeric architecture of recombinant HPIV2 N and provide a structural reference for RNA binding, inter-protomer organisation and future structure-guided engineering of the scaffold.
