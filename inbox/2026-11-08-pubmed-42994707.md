> [!abstract] Abstract
> Liver fibrosis (LF) is a dynamic process characterized by excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition driven by hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation. Inducing senescence in activated HSCs can limit fibrosis. The p53-p21 signaling axis is a key regulator of cellular senescence, however, how p53 activity is precisely controlled remains undefined. Liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) has emerged as an important stress-adaptive regulatory mechanism, driving the formation of stress granules (SGs) that regulate mRNA fate. Here, integrative analyses of human and murine datasets revealed that Sorcin expression is upregulated during fibrosis progression. Functional studies showed that Sorcin suppresses HSC senescence, as evidenced by reduced senescence markers, decreased SA-β-Gal positivity, and enhanced proliferative capacity. Mechanistically, elevated intracellular calcium drives Sorcin-mediated LLPS, enabling cooperation with the RNA-binding protein TIAR to nucleate SGs-like condensates. RNA immunoprecipitation, polysome profiling, dual-luciferase assays and EMSA demonstrated that TIAR directly binds TP53 mRNA and represses its translation. Consequently, TP53 mRNA is sequestered within SG-like condensates, attenuating p53 signaling and delaying HSC senescence. In vivo, AAV-mediated Sorcin knockdown enhances HSC senescence and alleviates LF in a CCl4-induced mouse model. This study reveals a phase separation-mediated translational control mechanism governing HSC senescence. By restraining senescence, Sorcin sustains fibrotic persistence, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for LF.
