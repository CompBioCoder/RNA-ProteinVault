> [!abstract] Abstract
> Transcriptome-wide methods to map RNA-protein interactions in living cells provide powerful tools to unravel the complexity of ribonucleoprotein (RNP) assemblies and their roles in post-transcriptional regulation. Here, we present three interconnected cross-linking and immunoprecipitation (CLIP)-based procedures, irCLIP-RNP, irCLIPv2 and Re-CLIP, that enable the identification and characterization of RNA-dependent associated proteins (RDAPs) in proximity to a target RNA-binding protein (RBP). After the construction of the infrared (IR) biotinylated DNA adapter, these methods combine UV cross-linking, partial RNase digestion, protein-specific immunoprecipitation and 3' DNA adapter ligation, followed by distinct downstream workflows: mass spectrometry for RDAP identification (irCLIP-RNP), RNA sequencing to map binding regions (irCLIPv2) or a second immunoprecipitation with sequencing for interaction validation (Re-CLIP). Custom bioinformatics pipelines analyze enriched RDAPs or significant binding sites, offering mechanistic insights into proximal RNA-protein interactions. Compared to conventional CLIP methods, these protocols resolve adjacent interactions, linking RBPs to their RDAPs with greater precision. The workflow is applicable across diverse contexts such as cell state transitions and stimulus responses. It requires expertise in RNA biochemistry, next-generation sequencing and computational analysis. The entire process, from cell harvesting to data analysis, can be completed in 7-10 d, providing a robust toolkit for investigating post-transcriptional regulatory networks.
