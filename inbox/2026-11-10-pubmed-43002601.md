> [!abstract] Abstract
> Accurate reference-free assessment of predicted RNA 3D structures is essential for large-scale RNA modeling, yet the impact of training data on deep learning-based quality predictors remains poorly understood. Here, we present a systematic cross-training analysis of three deep learning predictors for RNA 3D structure quality assessment, each originally developed on distinct training sets. By retraining each predictor on alternative (nonnative) datasets-including RNA3DQA, a new set created specifically for this project-and evaluating performance on independent RNA-Puzzles benchmarks, we systematically assess the relative contributions of model architecture and training data composition. Our results reveal pronounced differences in cross-dataset generalization, with predictors often performing better when trained on nonnative datasets than on their native ones. Notably, the impact of the training datasets varied across predictors, with RNA3DQA yielding the overall best accuracy and ranking stability results specifically for the ARES model. Native training sets yielded better performance for RNA3DCNN and lociPARSE. These findings demonstrate that the characteristics of the training data play a dominant, model-dependent role in shaping the performance and robustness of reference-free RNA 3D structure quality assessment methods. We conclude with practical recommendations for designing transferable training datasets and for benchmarking deep learning-based quality predictors in RNA structural bioinformatics.
