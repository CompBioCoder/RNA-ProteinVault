> [!abstract] Abstract
> RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are critical regulators of immune cell biology, yet their roles in innate lymphoid cell development remain largely undefined. We identify NOC4L, a conserved ribosome biogenesis factor with extraribosomal functions, as an essential RBP governing early natural killer (NK) cell development. NOC4L is enriched in NK progenitors (NKps) and dynamically upregulated by IL-15 signaling. Conditional deletion of NOC4L in mouse NKp cells severely disrupts NK cell homeostasis and causes a developmental blockade at the NKp stage, compromising anti-tumor immunity. Mechanistically, NOC4L exerts dual post-transcriptional functions: (1) as an RBP, it directly binds and stabilizes Nfil3 (also known as E4bp4) mRNA, encoding the master transcription factor for NK lineage commitment; and (2) independently of its RBP activity, it interacts with CDK9 to regulate cell-cycle progression. This study establishes NOC4L as the RBP coordinating post-transcriptional control and cell-cycle dynamics in early NK cell development, revealing potential targets for immune-based therapies.
