PubMed · 2026-11-10 · Cell Rep · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43013395/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Hepatic fibrosis is driven by excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition from activated hepatic stellate cells (HSCs). Although RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are emerging as key regulators of HSC activation, the role of RBM34 in liver fibrosis remains unexplored. Here we show that RBM34 is markedly upregulated in human and murine fibrotic livers, predominantly in activated HSCs, and is potently induced by TGFβ. RBM34 enhances fibrotic marker expression and HSC contractility, and HSC-specific knockdown attenuates liver fibrosis in vivo. Mechanistically, STAMP (survey targets by APOBEC-mediated profiling) and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) analyses identified lysyl oxidase-like 2 (Loxl2) mRNA as a direct RBM34 target. RBM34 interacts with DROSHA to promote nuclear export and translation of Loxl2 mRNA independently of DROSHA's canonical RNase III activity. Collectively, these findings uncover a non-canonical role for DROSHA in mRNA trafficking and establish the RBM34-DROSHA-LOXL2 axis as a driver of hepatic fibrogenesis, highlighting RBM34 as a potential therapeutic target.
