> [!abstract] Abstract
> Alternative splicing coordinates gene expression programs that shape the developing cerebral cortex. Here, we used nervous system-specific Rbm42 conditional knockout mice, RBM42-depleted human SH-SY5Y cells, RNA sequencing, RIP-seq, proteomics, and splice-switching rescue to define how the spliceosomal RNA-binding protein RBM42 supports cortical development. Rbm42 loss caused perinatal lethality, cortical thinning, defective lamination, reduced progenitor proliferation, increased apoptosis, and disrupted neuronal cytoskeletal organization. RBM42 bound and regulated cytoskeleton-associated transcripts, including NIN/Nin and NUMA1/Numa1, and its loss altered their splicing in human cells and mouse cortex. Restoring RBM42 corrected these splicing defects and improved proliferation and neurite growth. Selective correction of NIN exon 18 mis-splicing partially restored βIII-tubulin organization and proliferation but did not rescue apoptosis. These findings connect spliceosome-dependent isoform control with cytoskeletal remodeling during cortical development and provide a mechanistic framework for RBM42-associated neurodevelopmental disease.
