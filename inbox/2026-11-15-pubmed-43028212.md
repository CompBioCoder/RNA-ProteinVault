> [!abstract] Abstract
> Methyltransferases (MTs) are a functionally diverse class of enzymes that catalyse methyl group transfer from S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM) to DNA, RNA, and proteins, thereby regulating epigenetic programming, gene expression, and numerous disease processes. Dysregulation of these enzymes is implicated in a wide spectrum of human cancers and neurodegenerative disorders, making their accurate computational identification a priority in both basic research and drug discovery. We present GAC-BiT-CNN, a novel hybrid deep learning framework integrating Generative Adversarial Capsule Networks (GAC), Bidirectional Transformers (BiT), and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). The model leverages high-dimensional protein embeddings from four protein language models, including ProtGen, ESM-2, ProtT5-BFD, and ProtT5-XL, used individually, together with a fused feature set formed by concatenating the ProtT5-BFD and ProtT5-XL embeddings. All architectural components were optimised via systematic grid search hyperparameter tuning, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP)-based analysis was applied to the fused embeddings to identify the most discriminative feature dimensions. GAC-BiT-CNN with the fused feature set achieved 98.34% accuracy on the training set and 95.50% accuracy on the held-out test set, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 98.70% and a Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.901, surpassing all baseline models by a substantial margin. GAC-BiT-CNN provides a scalable, alignment-free framework with representation-level SHAP attribution for high-throughput methyltransferase identification, with direct applications in epigenomics, drug discovery, and cancer biology.
