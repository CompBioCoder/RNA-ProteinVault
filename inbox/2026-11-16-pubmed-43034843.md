> [!abstract] Abstract
> Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a frequent complication in hospitalized patients and is associated with increased mortality. Despite its relevance, no targeted therapies are available. RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are essential regulators of cellular function and have been implicated in AKI, but a comprehensive analysis of their role is lacking. We investigated RBP regulation in porcine kidneys subjected to ischemia-reperfusion injury following cardiopulmonary resuscitation. To assess evolutionary conservation, RBP profiles were also obtained from human kidney tissue. After ischemia-reperfusion injury, we found RBPs exhibiting altered RNA-binding capacity, including metabolic enzymes previously implicated in ischemic stress. These findings suggest that energy metabolism is linked to RNA regulation during kidney injury. Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury alters the RNA-binding landscape, affecting both regulatory and metabolic RBPs. The conservation of these proteins in human kidneys highlights their translational relevance and identifies RNA-protein interactions as potential therapeutic targets in AKI.
