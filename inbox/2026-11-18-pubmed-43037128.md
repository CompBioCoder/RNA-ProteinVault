> [!abstract] Abstract
> Gene expression evolution can take several forms, including changes in isoform usage, transcript abundance, and patterns of tissue- or cell type-specific expression. Using comparative and population-level transcriptomic analyses in the melanogaster subgroup of Drosophila, we previously documented the gain of Marf1 expression in the main cells of the D. melanogaster accessory gland, which produces most of the seminal fluid proteins in the species. Marf1, an RNA-binding protein, is ovary biased in the female and accessory gland biased in the male. It is required for female fertility, but its potential male functions are unknown. We investigated the possible male function of this gene using genetic, genomic, transcriptomic, and organismal analyses. We found that despite its very young expression phenotype in the male accessory gland, Marf1 has become integrated into the accessory gland gene regulatory network. We also found that its novel accessory gland expression is correlated with a fixed transposon fragment that is also associated with species-specific open chromatin in the accessory gland. Because Marf1 is located in the pericentromeric region of chromosome 2, which is riddled with repetitive DNA and characterized by low recombination rates and strong effects of linked selection, we hypothesize that novel accessory gland expression of this gene was deleterious and an incidental effect of centromeric hitchhiking effects, followed by compensation and integration of this gene into a novel gene regulatory network in the D. melanogaster accessory gland.
