> [!abstract] Abstract
> Abnormal DNA methylation in CpG islands (CGIs) is a hallmark of cancer, contributing to the stable silencing of tumor suppressor genes (TSG) and to epigenetic plasticity during tumor evolution. Although DNA methylation and RNA modifications are commonly regarded as distinct regulatory levels, emerging evidence supports the existence of functional interactions between the epigenome and the epitranscriptome. In this communication, we discuss a potential mechanism through which N6-methyladenosine (m6A)-modified RNAs that could influence CGI methylation via RNA-protein-chromatin interactions. m6A readers, including YT521-B Homology (YTH)-domain and RNA-binding proteins as well as fragile X syndrome messenger ribonucleoprotein 1 (FXR1), may link modified transcripts with chromatin-associated regulatory complexes and influence DNA methylation regulatory networks at specific genomic loci. However, there is currently no direct experimental evidence demonstrating the recruitment or modulation of DNMT/TET enzymes by m⁶A-dependent RNA-protein complexes in CGI-associated promoters. We therefore propose that this putative interface may represent an additional regulatory mechanism involved in the establishment or maintenance of aberrant promoter hypermethylation, with possible implications for tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. This connection between the epitranscriptome and the epigenome could provide a mechanistic framework for integrating the profiling of RNA modification and DNA methylation into cancer biology and the development of clinically relevant biomarkers.
