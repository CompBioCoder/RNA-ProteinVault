PubMed · 2026-11-02 · Front Cell Dev Biol · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43041732/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> The replication-independent histone variant H3.3 is encoded by two unlinked genes, H3f3a and H3f3b, that produce an identical protein. Nevertheless, published H3f3b mutant models report markedly divergent phenotypes, ranging from normal viability and fertility to growth restriction, infertility, and perinatal lethality. Here, we propose that these discrepancies arise partly from differences in allele architecture, particularly the position and transcript context of the native H3f3b 3'UTR. Across the available alleles, the 3'UTR is deleted, retained downstream of an engineered polyadenylation signal, preserved in its native context, recoupled to a reporter coding sequence, or displaced downstream of an intervening reporter cassette. Homozygous H3.3B-KO-mCherry mice lack H3.3B protein but retain and transcribe the native chromosomal 3'UTR directly downstream of mCherry and are viable and fertile. By contrast, H3.3B-HA-IRES-EYFP is a protein-producing knock-in in which the full-length native 3'UTR is retained downstream of both the H3.3B-HA and EYFP stop codons, with the IRES-EYFP sequence interposed between the H3.3B-HA and the 3'UTR; homozygous mice show reduced birth weight and early postnatal lethality. Compound offspring carrying both engineered alleles and lacking an unmodified wild-type H3f3b allele were recovered at Mendelian frequency. These observations support altered 3'UTR context and 3'-end processing as potential contributors to the divergent phenotypes, although genetic background and other features of allele construction remain relevant. We discuss possible regulation of the H3f3b transcript in cis, effects on other genes through shared microRNAs or RNA-binding proteins, and control of alternative cleavage and polyadenylation, and propose matched allelic tests of these mechanisms.
