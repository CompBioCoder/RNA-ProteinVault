> [!abstract] Abstract
> SARS-CoV-2 non-structural protein 9 (Nsp9) is an essential RNA-binding protein, yet the physical principles governing its RNA recognition remain poorly defined. Using NMR spectroscopy, small-angle X-ray scattering, dynamic light scattering, cell imaging, and viral yield-reduction assays, we show that Nsp9 preferentially engages conformationally heterogeneous RNA stem-oops and assembles through dimerization-assisted, multivalent interactions. Structured stem-loops with well-defined conformations and extended single-stranded RNAs exhibit only weak, localized binding. In contrast, the stem-loop-rich host 7SL S domain engages an extended interaction surface across the Nsp9 dimer. A 20-nt RNA hairpin bearing phosphorothioate and 2'-O-methyl modifications recapitulates this binding behaviour and promotes concentration-dependent formation of higher-order Nsp9 assemblies. NMR analysis indicates that this RNA retains stem-loop structure while populating a broader conformational ensemble than its unmodified counterpart. Disrupting the conserved GxxxG dimerization motif weakens binding and reduces assembly formation, demonstrating that dimerization assists integration of multiple weak RNA contacts into a cooperative interface. In cells, the modified RNA colocalizes with Nsp9 in cytoplasmic puncta and inhibits SARS-CoV-2 replication with nanomolar potency. Together, these results identify RNA conformational ensembles as important determinants of Nsp9 recognition, and suggest that RNA backbone modification can be used to engineer RNA-protein interactions through control of RNA conformational behaviour.
