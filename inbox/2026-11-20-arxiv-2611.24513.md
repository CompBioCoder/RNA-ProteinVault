**LaNell A. Williams, Amelia W. Paine, Kellianne Kornick, Rees F. Garmann, Vinothan N. Manoharan**  
arXiv · 2026-11-18 · physics.bio-ph · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24513)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> The protein capsids of many icosahedral RNA viruses can self-assemble in vitro in the absence of any host cell factors. For bacteriophage MS2 and related viruses, previous studies have focused on the role of specific RNA-protein interactions in driving the assembly process. However, the contribution of electrostatics remains unclear. We show that MS2 coat protein dimers can assemble into additional capsid layers around intact wild-type MS2 particles, even in the absence of free RNA. We characterize the size and morphology of the particles through transmission electron microscopy, gel electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering, and we use binding and infectivity assays to determine how the maturation protein affects layer formation. Our results are consistent with a heterogeneous nucleation-and-growth assembly pathway, in which the nucleation barrier is controlled by the protein concentration, and electrostatic attraction between the positively charged coat proteins and negatively charged capsid drives growth. Interestingly, the secondary layers rarely fully encapsulate the wild-type particles, suggesting that elastic energy may limit their growth. These results highlight the importance of electrostatics in the assembly of protein shells, even in systems such as MS2 that are known for their strong, specific RNA-protein interactions.
