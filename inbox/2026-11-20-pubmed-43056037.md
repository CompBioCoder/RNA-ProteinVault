> [!abstract] Abstract
> Guanine-rich RNA sequence binding factor 1 (GRSF1), a mitochondrial RNA-binding protein, plays a critical role in maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis and mitigating oxidative DNA damage. Whether GRSF1 contributes to cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury (CIRI) by regulating mitochondrial dynamics remains to be elucidated. Male C57BL/6 J mice underwent middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion (MCAO/R) to induce CIRI. Principal component analysis and enrichment analysis were employed to validate impairment in mitochondrial dynamics in CIRI mice. GRSF1 was then evaluated for its effects on neurological function and histology. The role of GRSF1 on mitochondrial dynamics following CIRI was examined through enrichment analysis, electron microscopy, and respiratory chain complex activity assays. The relationship between GRSF1 and mitofusin 2 (Mfn2) in mitochondrial fusion was further investigated. The regulatory mechanism of mitochondrial E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 1 (Mul1) on Mfn2 was assessed using co-immunoprecipitation and denaturing immunoprecipitation assays. CIRI mice were characterized by disrupted mitochondrial dynamics, and restoring this balance ameliorates pathological progression. GRSF1 improved neurological function in CIRI mice, an effect confirmed to associate with maintenance of neuronal mitochondrial dynamics. Mechanistically, GRSF1 promoted mitochondrial fusion by suppressing the expression or activity of Mul1, thereby reducing Mul1-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of Mfn2. Ultimately, GRSF1 attenuated CIRI by inhibiting neuronal mitochondrial dynamic imbalance via the Mul1/Mfn2 pathway. It uncovers GRSF1 as a novel neuroprotective regulator in CIRI and identifies the Mul1/Mfn2 pathway as a critical mechanistic pathway underlying its protective effects. Targeting GRSF1 may hold therapeutic potential by modulating mitochondrial dynamics in CIRI.
