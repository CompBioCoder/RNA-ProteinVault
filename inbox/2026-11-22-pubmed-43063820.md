> [!abstract] Abstract
> The lack of a licensed human vaccine for Toxoplasma gondii demands novel design strategies. This study aims to computationally design and structurally characterize an AI-optimized circular RNA (circRNA) vaccine candidate against this parasite, evaluating its macromolecular properties and predicted immunogenicity. An immunoinformatics pipeline identified conserved B‑cell, helper T‑lymphocyte (HTL), and cytotoxic T‑lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes from three key virulence antigens (RASP1, MYR3, GRA72). Selected epitopes were joined with optimized linkers and encoded into a self‑splicing circRNA backbone (T4 intron, IRES). Two design strategies were compared: conventional human codon‑optimization versus AI‑based refinement using the GEMORNA algorithm. Constructs were evaluated for codon adaptation index (CAI), secondary‑structure stability (minimum free energy, MFE), GC content, solubility, and antigenicity. Molecular docking against TLR7 and MHC‑II receptors assessed binding affinity. Finally, immune‑simulation profiled the predicted humoral and cellular immune response. The immunogenic vaccine construct showed a predicted binding affinity with TLR7 and MHC-II immune receptors. The AI‑optimized circRNA exhibited superior in silico properties, including a higher CAI (0.86 vs. 0.85) and a more stable secondary structure (MFE = ‑624.20 kcal/mol vs. ‑605.20 kcal/mol). Docking confirmed strong interactions with TLR7 and MHC‑II. Immune simulation projected a predicted immune-activation profile characterized by elevated immunoglobulins (IgG, IgM), significant lymphocyte proliferation, and increased secretion of IFN‑γ and IL‑2. The AI‑refined candidates show a promising predicted immune profile, warranting experimental validation and toxicity assessment for clinical translation. offering a rational design template for next‑generation anti‑parasitic circRNA vaccines. However, further experimental validation is needed to check the safety and immune activation ability of the designed vaccine against chronic T. gondii infection.
