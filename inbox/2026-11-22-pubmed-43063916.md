> [!abstract] Abstract
> Free energy perturbation (FEP) has become a cornerstone of structure-based drug design, yet its success has been built almost entirely on protein targets. Extending this physical rigor to RNA─an emerging and largely untapped therapeutic target class─confronts challenges that protein-ligand systems rarely impose: pronounced conformational flexibility, dense and highly anisotropic electrostatics, and ligand binding that frequently depends on direct metal coordination. Whether and when these challenges demand more sophisticated physical models has remained an open question. Here, we address it through a systematic comparison of the polarizable OPLS5 and fixed-charge OPLS4 force fields within an alchemical FEP framework, across three RNA-ligand systems of increasing complexity: (i) a fully resolved PreQ1 riboswitch with native-like fragments; (ii) a partially resolved PreQ1 riboswitch with synthetic ligands; and (iii) a group II intron with metal-coordinating inhibitors. We find that preparatory molecular dynamics is a crucial determinant of accuracy, with even short nanosecond-scale equilibration substantially improving ligand ranking by allowing the system to access functionally relevant conformations. Explicit polarizability becomes decisive precisely where the physics is hardest─recovering native-like architectures from incomplete structures, and converging charge-changing transformations at metal-coordination sites─while offering only marginal gains for well-resolved, structurally stable complexes. Together, these results define the conditions under which physical rigor is justified despite its higher computational cost and chart the potential for failures that still constrain free-energy methods applied to RNA. More broadly, our results offer a transferable framework for deploying physics-based affinity prediction against RNA target classes.
