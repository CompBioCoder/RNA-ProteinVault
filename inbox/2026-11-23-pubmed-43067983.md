PubMed · 2026-10-29 · Mol Ther Oncol · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43067983/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Dysregulation of RNA is closely associated with cancer initiation and promotion. Ribosomal protein S3 (RPS3), as a component of the small subunit 40S of the eukaryotic ribosome, functions as an RNA-binding protein involved in ribosome maturation and the initiation of translation. However, its role in pancreatic cancer biology and its potential as a therapeutic target remain unclear. In this study, we found that RPS3 knockdown strongly suppresses cell proliferation in multiple pancreatic cancer cell lines, SUIT-2, PK-1, and PANC-1 cells, in vitro. In a xenograft model, RPS3 knockdown inhibited tumor growth. Immunocytochemistry revealed a reduction in the proportion of Ki-67-positive pancreatic cancer cells following RPS3 knockdown. Flow cytometry demonstrated that RPS3 knockdown induced G2/M arrest, accompanied by reduced cyclin B1 protein expression. RNA-immunoprecipitation assay revealed RPS3 binds with leucine-rich repeat-containing G-protein coupled receptor 4 (LGR4) mRNA, an upstream regulator in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling. RPS3 knockdown reduced LGR4 protein expression and the nuclear translocation of β-catenin, accompanied by the suppression of c-Myc expression. LGR4 overexpression significantly rescued the suppression of cell proliferation induced by RPS3 knockdown. These findings indicate that RPS3 supports pancreatic cancer cell proliferation through the LGR4/Wnt/β-catenin signaling and highlights RPS3 as a potential therapeutic target in pancreatic cancer.
