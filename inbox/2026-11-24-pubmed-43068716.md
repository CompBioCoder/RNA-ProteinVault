> [!abstract] Abstract
> Gliomas are a group of aggressive tumours in the central nervous system (CNS) characterised by rapid growth, therapeutic drug resistance, and poor prognosis. There is evidence that post-transcriptional regulation is important in glioma development, with noncoding RNAs and RNA-binding proteins playing key roles. The different categories of ncRNAs, such as miRNAs, lncRNAs, circRNAs, and other RNA molecules, function in multiple cellular activities, including angiogenesis, metabolic rewiring, and drug resistance. Recent data suggest that RBPs and ncRNAs engage in a bidirectional regulatory process via RNA-protein interactions, in which RBPs govern ncRNA processing, maturation, transport, stabilisation, degradation, and functional activation, while ncRNAs modulate RBP activity. Dysregulation of RBPs through alterations in ncRNA-mediated pathways or cancer signalling pathways has been reported in glioma and there is also accumulating evidence for the relevance of epitranscriptomic modifications in altering RBP-ncRNA interactions. Several key RBPs, such as HuR, IGF2BPs, LIN28, hnRNPs, and members of the AGO protein family, have been implicated in regulating ncRNA pathways that contribute to tumourigenesis and drug resistance. In this review, we provide a comprehensive summary of current knowledge on the mutual regulation between RBPs and ncRNAs and its effects on the biogenesis and function of ncRNAs in gliomas. We also discuss the potential significance of manipulating these RBP-ncRNA regulatory mechanisms for future clinical applications in glioma diagnosis and therapy.
