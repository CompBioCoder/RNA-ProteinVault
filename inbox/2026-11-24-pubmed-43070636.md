> [!abstract] Abstract
> Accurate prediction of RNA 3D structures remains a central challenge in structural bioinformatics, necessitating reliable measures to quantify similarity between predicted models and experimentally determined reference structures. Currently, many scoring functions are available to assess different aspects of the tertiary structures of biomolecules. On the one hand, global measures average local irregularities, so that structures with distortions in different regions might generally be considered structurally similar. On the other hand, local conformational measures focus on specific inter-residue interactions and often fail to capture the broader topological context. Thus, there is a lack of methods capable of comprehensively assessing structural discrepancies across various spatial scales. To address this gap, we introduce Geometric Spherical Precision (GSP), a novel multi-scale score that assesses the quality of 3D RNA and RNP complex predictions. It integrates multiple partial scores computed from local geometric agreements into a single, easy-to-read, normalized score for ranking these predictions. We demonstrate that GSP reliably identifies models with comparable overall deviations, while maintaining strong agreement with global measures. These properties make it a useful tool for benchmarking and comparing 3D RNA structures and RNP complexes. The GSP script and evaluation results against state-of-the-art measures on the RNA-Puzzles and CASP15 datasets are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19608328. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.
