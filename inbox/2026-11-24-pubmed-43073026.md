**Anna C Hakes, Geraldine Seydoux**  
PubMed · 2026-11-23 · Annu Rev Genet · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43073026/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Germ granules are RNA-protein assemblies that segregate to the primordial germ cells (PGCs) in embryos. Germ granules are often considered cell fate determinants, yet not all animals use germ granules to specify germ cell fate. The function of germ granules is of renewed interest now that they are recognized as biomolecular condensates, cellular compartments that assemble without limiting membranes. We highlight common features of germ granules in Drosophila melanogaster, Caenorhabditis elegans, and Danio rerio, including a core shell architecture, complex mRNA composition, and concentration of PIWI-class Argonautes. These features suggest that a primary role of germ granules is to transmit condensate-based epigenetic programs across generations. Germ granules were also co-opted as vehicles for RNA localization by a small group of transcripts required to launch the germline, including Nanos. We suggest that this secondary function may have led to specializations in germ granule architecture to accommodate species-specific constraints on mRNA regulation.
