> [!abstract] Abstract
> Oncogenic, or "high-risk" human papillomaviruses (hr-HPVs) contribute to roughly 5% of cancers worldwide, including nearly all cervical carcinomas, a rising share of oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinomas (OPSCCs), and several other anogenital malignancies. Canonical models emphasize the hr-HPV oncoproteins E6 and E7, which inactivate the p53 and retinoblastoma (RB) tumor suppressor pathways to drive aberrant cell-cycle progression and transcriptional reprogramming. An expanded paradigm now recognizes that hr-HPVs extensively reconfigure host post-transcriptional gene regulation to reshape the proteome. Here we synthesize mechanistic advances showing that HPVs target multiple layers of RNA metabolism, including alternative splicing, polyadenylation, non-coding RNA networks, mRNA stability and decay, cap-dependent and cap-independent translation, and N6-methyladenosine (m6A) and related epitranscriptomic modifications. Loss of coupling between E6 and E7 expression and both E2 repression and 3'-untranslated region (UTR)-mediated decay, whether through integration, episomal amplification, or virus-host fusion, stabilizes oncogene transcripts. RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), including serine/arginine-rich (SR) proteins, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (hnRNPs), HuR (human antigen R or ELAV-like RNA-binding protein 1 [ELAVL1]), and cleavage and polyadenylation factors, are co-opted to coordinate viral gene expression with epithelial differentiation and to redirect host transcript fate. Non-coding RNAs, including host microRNAs (miR), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNA), and virally encoded species such as circular E7 (circE7), add further regulatory tiers. At the level of translation, HPV oncoproteins activate PI3K-AKT-mTORC1 and MNK-eIF4E signaling to favor synthesis of pro-oncogenic mRNAs. As the viral replication cycle is executed in a differentiating epithelium, we also consider the reciprocal problem of how the changing intracellular environment of the differentiating keratinocyte acts upon the virus. These changes include cell-cycle exit, withdrawal of cap-dependent translational capacity, remodeling of the tRNA pool, redistribution of RBPs, and a shifting stress and epitranscriptomic landscape. Additionally, we note how hr-HPVs have distributed sensors across epithelial layers to convert host differentiation into the timing signal for late gene expression. Together, these processes produce oncogenic translatomes that support proliferation, survival, immune evasion, and metabolic adaptation, and they expose actionable therapeutic vulnerabilities. Throughout, we indicate where supporting evidence derives from transformed cell lines and differentiating organotypic (raft) epithelial tissue cultures and remains to be validated in authentic, productively infected tissue.
