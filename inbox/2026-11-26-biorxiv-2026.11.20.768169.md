> [!abstract] Abstract
> Selective modulation of dynamic proteins within native multiprotein assemblies remains a major challenging for chemical biology. Here, we develop p47A1, the first DNA aptamer targeting the p97 adaptor p47 (NSFL1C) and uncover an unexpected allosteric mechanism of action. Target-immobilized SELEX identifies a highly convergent aptamer whose conformation is stabilized by Mg2+. Our comprehensive structural and biophysical characterization shows that p47A1 recognizes the SEP domain and adjacent SIM-SEP linker of p47 with low-micromolar affinity. Rather than simply occupying the p47 surface, aptamer binding remodels the intramolecular regulatory architecture of p47, disrupting intramolecular contacts and releasing the UBA domain into a highly dynamic state. Site-specific fluorescent labeling and mutational studies define the aptamer binding site. Importantly, reconstitution studies demonstrate that the aptamer engages p47 within the intact p47-p97 complex without disrupting the native complex or the basal p97 ATPase activity. Together, p47A1 provides a precise chemical biology probe for p47 and illustrates the potential of aptamers to target dynamic regulatory adaptors within native AAA+ protein assemblies.
