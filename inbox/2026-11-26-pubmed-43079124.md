> [!abstract] Abstract
> Pseudouridine(Ѱ) is one of the most abundant modified ribonucleotides that modulates RNA structure, stability, and RBP interactions. However, the functional organization of RBPs that interact with Ѱ-modified regions of mRNAs remains poorly understood. While transcriptome-wide mapping revealed pseudouridine modification sites, available eCLIP datasets for RBPs give an idea about the proteins that interact with those regions. These interactions are not systematically integrated to explore functions of pseudouridine mediated by RBPs. In our study, we intersected consistently found Ѱ sites in mRNAs across different studies and RBPs binding to those regions and used a stringent cut-off to choose only highly significant interactions. To explore the connectivity among these overlaps, we constructed a network of mRNAs and the RBPs whose binding peaks overlap their Ѱ-modified sites. To get an idea about the most important nodes in the network, we performed network analysis and detected hub nodes based on their connectivity. Observably, Ψ-modified mRNAs encoding mitochondrial electron transport chain components including MT-CO3, COX6A1, and C14orf2 and RBPs including RPS3, SND1, GRWD1 were among the most highly connected hub nodes in the network. In addition, we identified clusters of RBPs with distinct functions, including RNA metabolism and transport, as well as regulation of translation and cellular stress response pathways. Domain analysis showed that Ψ-associated RBPs frequently contain DEAD box RNA helicase and K homology (KH) domains, consistent with RNA binding and secondary structure remodelling. Our network-based approach establishes a functional landscape of RBP modules at pseudouridylated mRNA regions, providing a resource for investigating epitranscriptomic control of post-transcriptional regulation.
