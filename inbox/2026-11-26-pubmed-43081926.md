> [!abstract] Abstract
> Protein-RNA binding affinity quantifies how strongly an RNA-binding protein recognizes its RNA partner and is central to understanding post-transcriptional regulation and RNA-targeted therapeutic design. This prediction problem is difficult because the binding signal is not carried by protein or RNA sequences alone, but by chemically heterogeneous residue-base contacts formed at flexible three-dimensional interfaces. Existing sequence-centric models tend to compress a complex into global representations, whereas descriptor-based structural methods often encode only predefined interaction patterns. A remaining challenge is therefore to learn affinity from the local protein-RNA interface while retaining the contextual information provided by modern protein and RNA language models. We introduce HARP-RPA, an interface-aware HeteroPairFormer that models protein-RNA complexes through heterogeneous residue/base pairs. It combines pretrained protein/RNA language-model embeddings with relation-aware structural pair features that distinguish intra-protein, intra-RNA, cross-interface, and special-token relations. Token and pair representations are jointly refined, and the readout integrates global complex evidence with local interface signals. On the PRA310 and PRA201 five-fold benchmarks, HARP-RPA achieves PCC/SCC values of 0.621/0.633 and 0.580/0.598, respectively, with competitive RMSE and MAE. The learned representation also transfers to the mCSM-RNA mutation-effect benchmark, achieving RMSE/MAE of 0.962/0.839 and PCC/SCC of 0.563/0.581 after supervised fine-tuning. Stratified, hard-case, and ablation analyses further support its robustness and component contributions. Code is available at https://github.com/wbw176-netizen/HARP-RPA, with version v1.0.0 archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23486566. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.
