> [!abstract] Abstract
> The clinical success of mRNA vaccines during the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the development of RNA-based therapeutics and validated their translational potential. This review examines recent advances and remaining challenges in the application of RNA technologies for cancer therapy. A literature search of PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase was conducted from database inception to July 2026. We review emerging RNA modalities, including messenger RNA, small interfering RNA, circular RNA, self-amplifying RNA, RNA editing technologies, and combinatorial RNA interference strategies for cancer treatment. Particular emphasis is placed on advances in delivery systems, including non-viral nanocarriers, exosomes, chemical modifications, and tumor-targeting approaches designed to improve stability, specificity, and therapeutic efficacy. Early clinical studies across solid and hematologic malignancies are also summarized. RNA-based therapeutics have demonstrated encouraging preclinical and early clinical activity against cancer, but efficient and selective delivery remains the major barrier to broader clinical translation. The growing regulatory acceptance of RNA medicines and the adaptability of RNA design support the development of personalized therapies.Future advances will depend on improved delivery technologies, rational combination strategies, scalable manufacturing, and the integration of artificial intelligence to optimize RNA design and therapeutic development.
