**Juntao Ke, Maiqi Chen, Yunan Gao, Yehong Cao, Li Wan, Wenli Zhang, Wanmeng Mu**  
PubMed · 2026-11-26 · Crit Rev Biotechnol · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43098556/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Beyond traditional transcriptional regulation, synthetic biology is increasingly turning its focus to the direct manipulation of RNA molecules, leveraging RNA stability, localization, translation, and processing to impose dynamic, reversible, and resource-efficient control over gene expression. This review presents a comprehensive overview of the evolving RNA manipulation toolkit, ranging from fundamental molecular tools to sophisticated and programmable cellular systems. We categorize existing strategies into two primary paradigms: RNA-centric approaches (e.g., synthetic riboregulators) and protein-coupled systems (e.g., RNA-binding proteins and CRISPR-Cas effectors). A central theme of this review is the integration of these tools with functional modules, such as translation control mechanisms, RNA modification enzymes, and synthetic biomolecular condensates, to construct higher-order cellular systems capable of precise spatiotemporal regulation. We discuss the significant potential of these programmable RNA systems in circuit design, metabolic engineering, and RNA modification. Additionally, we critically evaluate challenges related to specificity, orthogonality, and host compatibility, and propose future directions focused on multiplexed RNA circuit design, modular standardization, and the development of closed-loop control architectures. This review aims to highlight the emerging utility of RNA manipulation tools and their integration into synthetic biology frameworks.
