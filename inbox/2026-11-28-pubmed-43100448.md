> [!abstract] Abstract
> Adenovirus (AdV) is a human DNA virus reliant on host RNA processing machinery. While research into AdV has yielded landmark discoveries in RNA biology, studies investigating the scope of AdV dependency on specific host RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are lacking. Here we performed optical pooled screening on a cell library where hundreds of RBPs are endogenously tagged. Pooled imaging followed by in situ barcode sequencing revealed a broad view of virus-induced host RBP relocalization patterns, including those recruited to viral replication compartments (VRCs). A targeted siRNA screen of relocalized RBPs demonstrated a requirement for the nuclear speckle (NS) core protein SON during virus infection. Depletion of SON resulted in reduced viral gene expression, impaired NS protein recruitment to early-stage VRCs, and impaired mobility of SRRM2 during infection. These findings suggest a crucial role of SON and NS functions in regulating viral gene expression and demonstrate the power of pooled tagging coupled to optical screening in investigating virus-host interactions.
