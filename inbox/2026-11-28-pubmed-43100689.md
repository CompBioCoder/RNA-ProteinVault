> [!abstract] Abstract
> Cytoplasmic aggregation of the nuclear RNA-binding protein TDP-43 is a defining pathology of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD) and is seen in many cases of Alzheimer's disease (AD), yet what initiates this process remains unknown. Here we identify an unexpected trigger: RNA from human endogenous retrovirus-K (HERV-K), an ancient retroviral element embedded in the human genome. Using CRISPR-engineered cells expressing Halo-tagged TDP-43 at endogenous levels, we show that HERV-K activation drives formation of cytoplasmic TDP-43 aggregates and functional loss of nuclear TDP-43, measured by aberrant cryptic exon inclusion. Surprisingly, cytoplasmic aggregation is preceded by the appearance of phosphorylated TDP-43 condensates in the nucleus, structures we term Pathological ERV-TDP-43-Associated Nuclear Spheres (PETANS). Live-cell imaging reveals that cytoplasmic inclusions originate directly from these nuclear bodies. This inverts the conventional model to suggest that pathological TDP-43 condensation begins in the nucleus, not the cytoplasm. Super-resolution imaging and structure-function mapping show that HERV-K RNA localizes within PETANS and drives their formation, with a minimal UG-rich non-coding element sufficient to induce the full cascade of nuclear and cytoplasmic pathology. Our findings establish an endogenous retroviral RNA as a direct molecular nucleator of TDP-43 proteinopathy.
