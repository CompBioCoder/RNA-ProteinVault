> [!abstract] Abstract
> Endogenous double stranded RNAs (dsRNAs) function as signal transduction molecules through interactions with host proteins. These interactions can induce signaling cascades implicated in normal homeostasis and diseases such as cancer. Therefore, the discovery of interactions between dsRNAs and dsRNA binding proteins (dsRBPs) is critical for comprehensive elucidation of signal transduction cascades. Discovery of dsRNA-dsRBP interactions remains hindered due to technical limitations that can include a lack of reliable reagents to enrich a given dsRBP or enrichment techniques that cannot identify direct dsRNA-dsRBP binding. As an alternative, we report the development of protein domain arrays to enable high-throughput discovery of direct interactions between dsRNAs and dsRBP 'reader' domains. We establish a modular antibody-independent approach coined 'dsRBP-seq' to identify putative dsRNAs. Validated dsRNAs are subsequently used to probe dsRBP domain arrays to discover multiple dsRNA binding domains that bind the same dsRNA. The establishment and application of this new technical approach reveals a domain-specific interaction of the dsRNA transcript encoding 26S proteasome complex subunit, Sem1, that binds with double-stranded RNA binding motif 3 of Staufen Double-Stranded RNA Binding Protein 2 (STAU2). Altogether, our workflow establishes a high-throughput approach to discover direct dsRNA-dsRBP interactions and the protein domains required for dsRNA binding.
