> [!abstract] Abstract
> Organophosphate pesticides are known to cause chronic neuronal dysfunction; however, mechanistic insights remain insufficient to establish disruption of neuronal homeostasis. This study examined the systems-level architecture underlying monocrotophos (MCP)-induced neurotoxicity and assessed the modulatory potential of quercetin in human SH-SY5Y neuronal cells. Label-free quantitative proteomics, Open Array-based microRNA (miRNA) and mRNA profiling, and targeted real-time PCR validation were performed to interrogate sub-cytotoxic doses of MCP (300µM, 72h) and quercetin (1µM, 24h). MCP exposure caused extensive neuronal remodelling, affecting mitochondrial function, redox homeostasis, protein synthesis and stress responses. Among 1,616 identified proteins, 284 were deregulated after MCP exposure. Enrichment analysis showed major changes in oxidative phosphorylation, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, antioxidant defences, ribosomal regulation, and the ubiquitin-proteasome system. MCP also altered miRNA circuits related to neurodegeneration, including miR-29, miR-34a, miR-146a and miR-181c, impacting oxidative stress, autophagy and apoptosis. Pathway analysis indicated disruptions in HIF-1, phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B, p53 and mitophagy signalling, showing coordinated network disruption. Quercetin significantly reduced these molecular effects in pre- and co-exposure. These findings characterise MCP neurotoxicity as an impaired network homeostasis similar to early neurodegenerative phenotypes and highlight quercetin as a possible early-stage intervention targeting toxicant-induced dysregulation in RNA-protein interactions.
