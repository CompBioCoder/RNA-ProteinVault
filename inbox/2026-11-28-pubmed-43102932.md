PubMed · 2026-11-27 · Sci Adv · [原文](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43102932/)

> [!abstract] Abstract
> Biomolecular condensates assemble, remodel, and disassemble in response to changing cellular conditions, but the mechanisms that drive these transitions remain poorly understood. Germ granules-RNA-rich condensates essential for germ cell development and fertility-exhibit developmental plasticity, yet how this behavior is controlled is not known. Using in situ proximity labeling, quantitative proteomics, and live super-resolution imaging, we show that Drosophila germ granules are remodeled into multiphasic condensates after primordial germ cell formation. Remodeling is driven by the conserved RNA binding protein Nanos, translated from germ granule-localized nanos messenger RNA (mRNA). As Nanos accumulates above a critical threshold, it condenses to form a coat surrounding the germ granules and recruits the CCR4-Not deadenylase complex and other P-body components. Transition to this hybrid germ granule-P-body-like state alters condensate organization and physical properties. Formation of the Nanos coat coincides with the release of nanos mRNA from germ granules while promoting the retention and relocalization of Cyclin B mRNA from the granule interior to the Nanos phase. These findings identify an autoregulatory developmental timer in which Nanos drives condensate remodeling to control the localization and fate of its own and other mRNAs during germ cell specification.
